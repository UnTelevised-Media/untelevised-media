It is a war against anyone who opposes the machinery of state violence.

This is the full picture — every defendant, every year, every lie the government told to put them in cages, and the nationwide crackdown the Prairieland case was designed to accelerate.

## How We Got Here: What Actually Happened

On the night of July 4, 2025, a group of activists from Dallas-Fort Worth arrived at the Prairieland ICE Detention Center in Alvarado, Texas, about 40 miles south of Dallas. They intended to hold what they described as a *"noise demonstration"* — setting off fireworks to let the people detained inside know they were not forgotten. Independence Day fireworks, at an immigration prison, in the country that invented both.

As [we've reported extensively on ICE's killing machine](https://untelevised.media/articles/doj-conspiracy-charges-ice-protesters-federal-crime-solidarity/), the conditions inside detention centers are monstrous. People die in custody. [Private prison companies like GEO Group](https://untelevised.media/articles/geo-group-guard-shoots-emma-landis-aurora-ice-facility-protest/) guard these facilities with impunity. The federal government [has poured $529 million into building new detention centers](https://untelevised.media/articles/usd529-million-for-cages-ice-s-new-colorado-detention-center-and-the-private-prison-industry/) while cutting food assistance for the poor. People who show up to say *this is wrong* are now being treated as terrorists.

Some in the Prairieland group broke off and vandalized vehicles and a guard shack — spray-painting slogans, slashing tires, breaking a security camera. Nathan Baumann, who admitted to the vandalism, received the lightest sentence in the entire case: under two years. Those who merely attended the protest received 50.

When Alvarado Police Lt. Thomas Gross arrived on scene and drew his weapon, Song opened fire with an AR-15. The bullet entered above Gross's collarbone and exited through his upper back. Gross was airlifted to a hospital and released within 24 hours. He has fully recovered.

The critical dispute: Song says he fired at the ground as suppressive fire because Gross had drawn his gun on another fleeing protester — a fact Gross admitted under cross-examination. The defense argued a ricochet struck the officer. Judge Pittman refused to allow the ricochet argument. **No ballistic report was submitted at trial. No medical evidence was introduced.** The prosecution's case that Song intended to murder Gross rested entirely on inference.

## Autumn Hill Speaks: *"I Am a Political Prisoner"*

On July 25, 2026, Uncloseted Media published [Autumn Hill's first video interview from Johnson County Jail](https://www.unclosetedmedia.com/p/autumn-hill-speaks-from-jail-after). What she said matters — because the state that sentenced her to 50 years does not want you to hear it.

Hill — also known by her drag name Lady Hell — is a neuroqueer self-advocate who lived at what her housemates called the *"Big Gay House"* in South Dallas, a home purchased by Meagan Morris and her friend Taylor specifically to provide shelter to housing-insecure queer and trans people. Hill describes the house as an intentional community space. She met her housemates through activist circles. She dated first Shelby, then Meagan.

She attended the Prairieland protest and left hours before the shooting. The trial transcript confirms this. The FBI and Alvarado police witnesses were unable to produce evidence placing her at the scene when shots were fired. She was acquitted of attempted murder and firearms charges.

None of this mattered.

*"I never believed for a minute leading up to the trial that Champagne had attempted to murder someone in cold blood,"* Hill told Uncloseted Media. *"Champagne is one of the most calm, level-headed and principled people I know."*

> On the raid itself: *"They're ordering me to stay where I am, and then they're ordering the person in the red dress to come forward... They order me to turn around and walk backward toward them, and then they put me in handcuffs, and then they sit me on the pavement outside of my house and they leave me there for like 30 minutes under guard."*

> On antifa: *"There's no membership in antifa. They want to create a narrative of leftist political violence as being as much or more of a problem in the United States than, you know, right-wing stochastic terrorism. You know, they want to create a false equivalency which the data shows just doesn't exist."*

> On her 80 days in solitary confinement — what the jail calls *"administrative segregation"*: *"These cells are very small, like the size of a bathroom, right? So you've got like a bunk, you've got a little table, and you've got a shower and a toilet..."*

Hill is one of three trans defendants in the case. Since her arrest, she has been incarcerated in men's facilities. The Trump administration's [new counterterrorism strategy](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-USCT-Strategy-1.pdf), released in May 2026, explicitly highlights *"radically pro-transgender"* people as a threat — despite no evidence that trans people are disproportionately likely to commit acts of violence. [The administration's narrative](https://www.unclosetedmedia.com/p/trump-falsely-links-trans-people) linking trans identity to terrorism is a fabrication, but it is a fabrication with 50-year prison sentences behind it.

## The Weapons of a Noise Demonstration

The entire trial was a stretch of *“evidence” *well beyond the realms of imagination.** **The government's evidence of a ***"terrorist cell"*** is worth examining in detail, because it reveals the absurdity — and the danger — of what has been normalized.

<!-- IMAGE alt="Government evidence exhibit: Fireworks exploding near no one." asset="image-ef0d2f686f7eb461d2390c21d865131ad092aaca-400x169-webp" -->

  - **The "explosives":** Fireworks. Commercial fireworks. The kind Americans set off on every street corner on July 4th. No people were injured by the fireworks. No buildings were damaged by the fireworks. They were fired into the air.
  - **The "material support for terrorists":** Providing *"training,"* transportation, and *"personnel"* — meaning the protesters themselves — for the commission of the federal crime of Riot. The *"riot"* consisted of setting off fireworks, Baumann's vandalism, and forming what prosecutors called a *"black bloc."*
  - **The *****"black bloc"*****:** Wearing dark clothing. This is a tactic, not an organization — commonly associated with anarchism as a means of showing political solidarity and evading surveillance. Police bodycam footage from the trial showed that arrestees were not uniformly dressed in black. Savanna Batten was arrested in a tank top. Ines Soto was wearing blue work pants. Song was wearing a neon green mask.

  - **The expert witness:** Kyle Shideler of the Center for Security Policy, a group the Southern Poverty Law Center describes as a *"conspiracy-oriented mouthpiece for the anti-Muslim movement."* Shideler testified that antifa members typically espouse *"strong anarchist ideology or some of these other non-authoritarian socialist ideologies."* He has published pieces warning of an impending *"anarcho-communist insurgency."*

The case was announced with statements from Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche, FBI Director Kash Patel, Acting ICE Director David J. Venturella, and U.S. Attorney Ryan Raybould. The full weight of the national security apparatus, mobilized for a noise demonstration at a county detention center.

Consider the legal precedents now established:

Enrique Tarrio, the Proud Boys leader convicted of seditious conspiracy for his role in an armed attack that killed five people and injured 140 officers, received **22 years**. He was pardoned by Trump on his first day back in office. Stewart Rhodes, the Oath Keepers founder convicted of the same charge, got **18 years**. Pardoned.

People who attended a protest with fireworks and spray paint are now serving sentences that are **five to ten times longer** than the leaders of an actual armed insurrection — an insurrection whose participants the President personally freed.

Sufia Khalid, deputy director of the National Security Criminal Defense Center at the Muslim Legal Fund of America, drew the comparison directly: *"There were people who were up in arms about the Jan. 6 prosecutions... but we had far more serious, egregious conduct in those cases, and in the vast majority of those 1,500 cases, they didn't seek the terrorism enhancement."*

The terrorism enhancement — the legal mechanism that turns a 15-year sentence into 50 — was available in every January 6 case. Prosecutors chose not to use it. For people who actually breached the Capitol. For people who actually chanted about hanging the Vice President. For people who actually beat police officers with flagpoles.

But for a noise demonstration at an ICE detention center? The full weight of the terrorism apparatus, deployed without restraint.

## Appeals and the Road Ahead

All 15 defendants are pursuing appeals. But appeals take years. The defendants are sitting in cages now. Autumn Hill is in a men's facility, staring down half a century for leaving a protest early. Elizabeth and Ines Soto — a married couple — are both serving 50 years for running a zine press and attending a book club. Daniel Estrada is serving 30 years for moving a box of his own poetry.

Additionally, the State of Texas intends to hold its own trials in connection with this protest. The federal sentencing may not be the end. It may be the beginning.
