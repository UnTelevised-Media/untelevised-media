On June 14, Alexandra Bravo — the Ecuadorian prosecutor assigned to investigate U.S. drone strikes on three civilian fishing boats and the survivors' accounts of abduction and torture at the hands of American forces — was shot dead in the coastal city of Manta. Her sister, Olinda Bravo, was killed alongside her. The hitman escaped on a black motorcycle. The police detail assigned to protect Bravo was nowhere to be found.

Bravo was the 26th member of Ecuador's justice system to be assassinated since 2020. She was the second investigator of state crimes murdered that same week. But her case is different. Alexandra Bravo wasn't investigating local corruption or gang violence. She was investigating the United States government.

## The Three Boats

Bravo's caseload included the Fiorella, the Negra Francisca, and the Don Maca — three Ecuadorian fishing vessels struck by explosives in what the Pentagon claims is a counter-narcotics campaign called Operation Southern Spear. The operation has killed at least 221 people since September 2025. Congress has never authorized military force in the region.

**Key facts about the three vessels:**
- **Fiorella:** Set sail from the port of Jaramijó on January 13, 2026 with ten crew members. Eight fishermen remain missing. Two survivors in a skiff reported seeing the Fiorella engulfed in smoke surrounded by American assets — drones, aircraft, and a U.S.-flagged patrol ship.
- **Negra Francisca:** Bombed on March 17, 2026. Its crew was abducted, blindfolded, and starved for eight days by men in U.S. military uniforms aboard a U.S.-flagged patrol ship. Survivors were abandoned in El Salvador before eventually being returned to Ecuador.
- **Don Maca:** Bombed on March 26, 2026. Its 20 crew members experienced the same pattern of drone strike, abduction, and torture before being dumped in El Salvador.

There is no evidence that any of the 44 fishermen aboard these vessels were involved in drug trafficking. Medical experts found injuries consistent with explosions and torture — including 70 percent vision loss and feet torn to flesh and bone.

## A Prosecutor Under Siege

Bravo faced obstruction from every direction. According to reporting by Drop Site News, a source inside the Manta police department said Bravo was under direct pressure from superiors at the Attorney General's office to treat the U.S. attacks exclusively as "cases of disappearance" — to strip the investigation of any reference to American involvement in bombings or torture.

She was told to close all lines of inquiry after 36 of the 44 missing men were found alive, despite arriving with injuries that medical experts confirmed were consistent with their accounts of explosions and torture.

A senior researcher with Human Rights Watch who interviewed Bravo recalled that the prosecutor faced "internal challenges within the institution itself to advance the investigation." Bravo repeatedly sought cooperation from the Manta Port Captaincy and other agencies responsible for the ships' rescue. None responded to her requests.

Darwin Pico-Flores, an eyewitness aboard a nearby boat during the Negra Francisca bombing, called the Manta Port Captaincy requesting emergency assistance. *Nobody came.* When Drop Site News called the port for comment in April, officials hung up after learning the call was from journalists.

Fernando Bastias, a human rights attorney with Ecuador's CDH and member of the Geneva-based World Organization Against Torture, told Drop Site: *"I can't make any assertion regarding it being a political assassination, but evidently, when a prosecutor is killed it's because of the investigation they're conducting. That's something we've learned from our experience in Ecuador."*

## Silenced Before She Could Finish

In the days leading up to her death, Bravo was relocated to the Attorney General's office in Montecristi, a neighboring municipality. Her caseload changed. She was no longer in charge of investigating the three boat attacks. Her replacement was explicitly instructed not to speak to the press. The Attorney General's office told Drop Site the investigation was sealed and refused to explain why Bravo was transferred.

As of their last meeting on May 8, the Human Rights Watch researcher confirmed Bravo was still in the initial stages of inquiry.

The timing is damning. On June 13 — the day before Bravo's murder — Reps. Joaquin Castro (D-Texas) and Bill Keating (D-Mass.) published a letter to Secretary of War Pete Hegseth, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and Coast Guard Commandant Kevin Lunday demanding a full audit of U.S. government involvement in the attacks. Despite a July 10 deadline, none of the agencies have issued a public response.

## The Cover-Up in Plain Sight

Both governments have worked to ensure this investigation goes nowhere.

Under Ecuador's right-wing president Daniel Noboa — a Miami-born billionaire who has granted diplomatic immunity to all U.S. military and Department of Defense personnel through 22 martial law decrees — U.S. forces operating in Ecuador are legally untouchable. As Bastias put it: *"The Executive Branch has implemented a series of legal frameworks that leave these foreign state agents immune from prosecution."*

The U.S. maintains it never interacted with any of the three boats. A Coast Guard spokesperson told The Guardian the agency's only involvement was deploying the USCGC Bertholf on a search-and-rescue mission after receiving a MAYDAY call about the Negra Francisca — and finding nothing.

But the evidence contradicts the official story. Drop Site News, working with Airwars OSINT analyst and former U.S. Marine Tomi McCluskey, obtained vessel tracking data placing the Bertholf near the Negra Francisca around the time of the bombing — before the Coast Guard boat turned off its transponder. NASA thermal imagery corroborates the attack. Video footage shows U.S. Coast Guard activity in the vicinity. Police interviews with eyewitnesses confirm U.S. involvement.

The UN Committee on Enforced Disappearances demanded that the U.S. provide information on the whereabouts of the eight missing Fiorella crew members by April 27. The United States has not responded.

## Operation Southern Spear: A Campaign of Extrajudicial Killing

What happened to the Fiorella, Negra Francisca, and Don Maca is not an aberration. It is policy.

Operation Southern Spear is the Trump administration's drone bombing campaign targeting boats in the Caribbean Sea and eastern Pacific. At least 66 strikes have been carried out since September 2025. The administration claims all targeted vessels were carrying drugs and all 221 people killed were involved in trafficking.

Amnesty International has characterized the campaign as "a practice of extrajudicial executions and other violations of human rights that must cease immediately." Legal experts have stated that even if the bombed vessels were transporting drugs — which there is no evidence of in the three Ecuadorian cases — military attacks against civilians for alleged involvement in drug trafficking violate international law.

Congress has never authorized military force in the region. Trump has claimed the U.S. is in an armed conflict with Latin American drug cartels, but that declaration exists only in his own rhetoric.

## A Pattern of Impunity

This is not the first time the U.S. military has used the waters off Ecuador's coast as a killing ground. From 1999 to 2009, the U.S. operated Forward Operating Location Manta — a military base at Manta's Eloy Alfaro Airport used for aerial surveillance and counter-narcotics operations. The base was established precisely because of a history of U.S. military sinking boats off the coast of Manta. Ecuador's constitutional ban on U.S. military presence, ratified by 60 percent of voters as recently as November 2025, was originally introduced to prevent another Manta base.

Noboa brushed aside that constitutional ban. He authorized the first FBI office on Ecuadorian soil. He assisted a U.S. Embassy effort to falsely frame members of Colombia's left-wing government ahead of elections. He collaborated with the U.S. in the bombing of innocent civilian farmworkers along the Colombian-Ecuadorian border.

Notably, boats from Noboa's own family shipping corporation were caught trafficking £26 million of cocaine. That detail has not deterred the State Department from naming him a key partner in Trump's War on Drugs.

Mónica Palacios, a National Assembly lawmaker who announced plans to bring the Fiorella case before the United Nations, faced months of political retaliation. Last Friday she reported receiving death threats against herself and her one-year-old son, along with footage of the outside of her New York home. She directly blamed President Noboa.

## The Families Still Waiting

The survivors and families of the victims have received nothing but closed doors. As Drop Site reported, they say *authorities shut the door in our face.* Maria, the wife of one of the fishermen, told Drop Site her mother-in-law died awaiting her son's return. She was struggling with severe depression and stopped eating in the weeks leading up to her death.

Former Ecuadorian Foreign Minister Guillaume Long said U.S. military presence in Ecuador is *"a clear violation of our people's sovereignty and popular will."* He asked: *"How far does the 'war on drugs' go? Who is held accountable for the lives of Ecuadorian fishermen?"*

Diego Vintimilla Jarrín, formerly a lawmaker with Ecuador's National Assembly and now a spokesperson for Ecuador's Communist Party, told Drop Site: *"Noboa's government is the most anti-sovereign agenda we have had since the start of democracy. I have no proof of who murdered the prosecutor but the fact that she was investigating certain elements and that they later ceased to seem important also says a lot about the underlying issues."*

## Congress Speaks — The Pentagon Doesn't Listen

On July 25, Rep. Jesús "Chuy" García (D-IL) delivered the sharpest congressional statement yet on the case:

*"For almost a year, the U.S. government has been illegally bombing fishing boats and killing people without evidence of wrongdoing. And last month, Alexandra Bravo, the Ecuadorian prosecutor investigating boats that were attacked or disappeared, was murdered. This is not a coincidence. There must be an independent investigation."*

García's demand followed an earlier letter sent in May by 20 House progressives — led by García, Greg Casar (D-TX), and Sara Jacobs (D-CA) — demanding answers from Hegseth about alleged human rights violations during U.S.-Ecuador military operations. That letter warned that continued U.S. security assistance to Ecuadorian forces implicated in torture and extrajudicial killings would violate the Leahy Laws.

The Pentagon, Coast Guard, and U.S. Southern Command have all denied any knowledge or responsibility. None have responded to the Castro-Keating letter's July 10 deadline. The silence is the policy.
