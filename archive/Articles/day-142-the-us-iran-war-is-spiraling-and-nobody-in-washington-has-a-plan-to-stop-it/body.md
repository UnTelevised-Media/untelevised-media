## How We Got Here: From "Epic Fury" to Endless War

The war began on February 28, 2026, when the United States and Israel launched joint airstrikes codenamed *"Operation Epic Fury"* — a decapitation strike that killed Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, Defense Minister Aziz Nasirzadeh, IRGC commander Mohammad Pakpour, and at least 40 other senior Iranian officials. The strikes were launched amid US-Iran negotiations over Iran's nuclear program, negotiations that were supposedly ongoing when the missiles hit.

As we documented in our analysis of [Erasing Palestine: How Trump, Netanyahu, and the Board of Peace Are Completing the Nakba](https://untelevised.media/articles/erasing-palestine-how-trump-netanyahu-and-the-board-of-peace-are-completing-the-nakba/), the Trump administration has pursued a strategy of maximum escalation across the Middle East — and Iran was always the crown jewel. The war justification cycled through pretexts: preventing nuclear weapons, responding to crackdowns on protests, and eventually, the open admission that this was about taking Iran's oil and gas resources.

A two-week ceasefire began April 8, followed by an Islamabad Memorandum signed June 17 that lifted dual blockades. By June 28, both sides agreed to stop exchanging attacks. That deal lasted 10 days. On July 8, the ceasefire collapsed after mutual attacks, and the fighting has escalated every day since.

### The Regional Humanitarian Fallout

The economic damage extends far beyond gas prices at the pump. Iran's closure of the strait triggered what the United Nations Development Programme called a systemic collapse of the Gulf Cooperation Council economic model:

- **Food crisis:** 70% of GCC food imports disrupted; consumer prices spiked 40–120% across the region
- **Water crisis:** Iran has struck desalination plants in Kuwait — the source of 99% of that country's drinking water
- **Aviation shutdown:** Emirates, Qatar Airways facing near-total cessation; global air travel disrupted
- **European energy shock:** Dutch TTF gas benchmarks nearly doubled to over €60/MWh; UK inflation expected to breach 5%; ECB warned of stagflation
- **GDP losses:** UNDP estimates $120–194 billion in lost economic growth across Arab nations

QatarEnergy was forced to declare force majeure on all LNG exports in March — a move that sent shockwaves through European energy markets already strained by historically low gas storage levels following a brutal winter.
