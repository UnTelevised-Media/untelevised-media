Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ) went on NBC's Meet the Press on Sunday and said what growing numbers of Americans — and the rest of the world — have been watching in real time: **Donald Trump's corruption is unprecedented. There is nothing to compare him to.**

The numbers released last week in a 927-page financial disclosure from the US Office of Government Ethics confirm it. Trump pulled in more than $2.2 billion last year — from real estate, golf resorts, branded merchandise, licensing deals, legal settlements, and above all, cryptocurrency ventures that have turned the presidency into a profit center for the Trump family and their foreign benefactors.

## The $500 Million UAE Crypto Deal

Four days before Trump's second inauguration, associates of an Abu Dhabi royal — Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan, the UAE's national security adviser, known in US intelligence circles as the "Spy Sheikh" — bought a 49% stake in World Liberty Financial, the Trump family's cryptocurrency joint venture with Trump's Middle East envoy Steve Witkoff. The price: roughly $500 million. The deal was done in secret.

Then came the quid. Within months, the Trump administration took at least 10 actions benefiting the UAE, most notably reversing decades of national security objections to allow the sale of leading-edge Nvidia AI chips to the Emirates. National security officials had opposed the move, fearing that China would ultimately gain access to the chips through UAE intermediaries. The White House overruled them.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren called it what it is: a potential *"pay-to-play"* scheme. Sen. Chris Murphy (D-CT) called it *"mind-blowing corruption"* and declared the White House *"a non-stop corruption machine."* Five senators, including Warren and Murphy, wrote to Senate committees on June 23 demanding hearings into the deal.

Richard Briffault, a law professor at Columbia University, described the arrangement without euphemism:

> *“The situation of a major investment by a foreign power in a major company that the president has a major stake in, that creates a structural conflict of interest.”*

## The Meme Coin Cashout

World Liberty Financial — the company the UAE bought half of — generated more than $500 million from sales of *"governance tokens"* in Trump's first year back in office. CIC Digital LLC, another Trump-linked firm, pulled in more than $600 million from Trump-branded meme coins launched days before the inauguration. Investors who bought in were, in the words of California Gov. Gavin Newsom, were *"rug-pulled."* Trump got richer.

Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz called him *"the most corrupt president in American history."* Illinois Lt. Gov. Juliana Stratton wrote that Trump's *"infinite greed is disgusting,"* adding: *“Donald Trump uses the office of the president to make billions while American families struggle to afford their basic needs.”*

Trump waved off the scrutiny. *I made a lot of money before I became president,* he told reporters. The White House has long maintained that Trump's businesses are walled off from his official duties and run by his adult sons. No one believes this. The businesses operate in the same building as the White House's Middle East policy.

## The Foreign Property Portfolio

The disclosures also detailed Trump's overseas real estate empire — a portfolio that directly overlaps with US foreign policy. Among the payments:

- **UAE property:** $10.4 million
- **Saudi Arabia property** (built by a developer close to the ruling family): $9 million
- **Romania (Bucharest) property:** $5 million
- **Qatar property:** $5 million

These are not coincidences. Saudi Arabia and the UAE are negotiating with the US over tariffs, military aid, and AI technology transfers. Trump is personally collecting millions from the same governments his administration is doing business with. The Constitution's Emoluments Clause exists precisely to prevent this. Trump has rendered it a dead letter.

The latest spectacle: Trump took his first flight aboard the new Air Force One, a Boeing 747 gifted to the US by Qatar, en route to a presidential library dedication in North Dakota. The president called it *"the best plane ever built."* A foreign government gave the president of the United States a custom-built aircraft, and this is never front-page news.

## The $1.8 Billion Slush Fund

Perhaps the most brazen grift of Trump's second term: the $1.776 billion *"anti-weaponization"* fund, a proposed DOJ initiative that would have created a massive pool of taxpayer money to pay settlements to Trump's allies — including January 6 defendants and insurrectionists pardoned by the president himself.

The fund was exposed in May 2026 by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD), who released a fact sheet detailing 10 reasons why the scheme was unconstitutional, illegal, and a fraud on the court. Trump had reportedly promised mass pardons before leaving office; the slush fund would have ensured his allies were compensated for their loyalty with public money.

After public backlash, the DOJ claimed it was abandoning the plan. But White House officials filed a court document suggesting they were trying to create the fund anyway. Senate Republicans refused to formally block it. The Atlantic called it *"worse than stealing."*

## The Reflecting Pool Fiasco

If you want to understand how this administration operates, look at the Reflecting Pool. Trump ordered a $16 million renovation of the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool in Washington, DC — a project that descended into farce when the drained pool filled with toxic green algae, killing thousands of fish and creating a public health hazard on the National Mall.

The New Republic reported that arrests have been made in connection with the renovation disaster. The Atlantic described it as a 13-step fiasco that *"says everything about how the administration operates"* — vanity projects, no oversight, public money wasted, accountability nowhere.

## The Legal Settlement Cash

Trump also pocketed more than $86 million from five separate legal settlements with media and social media companies including ABC, CBS, YouTube, Meta, and X. These were settlements for defamation cases and other disputes — effectively, corporations paying the president to stop suing them. The chilling effect on press freedom is incalculable.

## The Most Corrupt President in History

There is a word for a head of state who personally profits from foreign investments timed to his inauguration, who sells national security-sensitive technology to the country that just paid his family half a billion dollars, who proposes a taxpayer-funded slush fund for his insurrectionist allies, who collects millions from the same governments he negotiates with, and who takes a custom airplane from a Gulf monarchy as a gift.

The word is corruption. Not in the abstract, not as a political allegation, but as a documented, numbered, receipt-backed fact pattern that makes every previous presidential ethics scandal look like a parking ticket.

> *There's nothing to compare him to. He spends most of his time trying to figure out how to enrich himself and his family members and his friends. It is corruption that we've never seen before, and it's hurting the American people.
> *— Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ), Meet the Press, July 27, 2026

The American people can't afford their lives. Trump made $4.7 million last year from Trump-branded watches alone. He made $600 million from meme coins. He made $500 million from a UAE crypto deal that may have cost the United States its edge in artificial intelligence.

This is not a presidency. This is a shakedown.
